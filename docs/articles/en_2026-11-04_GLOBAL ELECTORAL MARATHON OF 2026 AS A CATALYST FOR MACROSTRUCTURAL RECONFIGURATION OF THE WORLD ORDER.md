ABSTRACT

**This monograph provides a comprehensive, neo-institutional, and geoeconomic analysis of the global electoral cycle of 2026, examining its impact on planetary security, resource allocation, and technological sovereignty.** The study decodes the systemic implications of the U.S. midterm elections (November 3, 2026), which resulted in a decisive victory for the Democratic Party, establishing total legislative control over both chambers of the U.S. Congress and reducing the Donald Trump administration to an institutional "lame duck" operating under **Scenario B ("Mr. Veto")**.

The research synchronizes these domestic American shifts with structural ruptures across Europe—specifically the collapse of the Franco-German-Spanish _FCAS_ sixth-generation fighter project and Germany's pivot toward the UK-led _GCAP_ alliance, which has effectively split the continent into two divergent technopolitical axes: the autarkic "Latina Axis" (Paris) and the integrated "Northern Axis" (London/Berlin). Concurrently, the paper evaluates the limits of conventional deterrence in the Middle East following the unpublicized U.S. _CENTCOM_ kinetic strike on Iran’s Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, analyzing Iran’s asymmetric response matrix and its newly consolidated threshold nuclear status under the complete dismantling of UN Resolution 2231.

Finally, the study deconstructs the strategic adaptation of the Russian Federation and the People's Republic of China, demonstrating how Moscow and Beijing are migrating the "Energy Triangle" (Russia-USA-Europe) into the "deep shadow" of _OFAC_ temporary waivers while exploiting the political paralysis in Washington to build non-dollar clearing frameworks and advance "grey-zone" operations, as evidenced by the PRC's maritime chokehold near the Pratas (Dongsha) Islands and the systemic supply-chain infiltration of Taiwanese defense consortia (_Align Corp_). Utilizing the analytical framework of **transactional realism**, the author projects three macro-political scenarios for the evolution of the global architecture up to 2035, concluding that real power in the current era belongs not to resource owners or end consumers, but to the operators of transactional pipelines, tech stacks, and algorithmic standards.
